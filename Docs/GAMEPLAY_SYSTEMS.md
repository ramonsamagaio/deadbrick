# DEADBRICK gameplay systems blueprint

## 1. World generation

Generation is deterministic and layered. Districts define probabilities and rules, then roads carve blocks, lots choose building archetypes, and each building generates floor plans and utility/service spaces. Later passes place damage, abandoned vehicles, barricades, loot, survivor traces and zombie populations.

Target district set: downtown, residential, low-income housing, suburbs, commercial strips, industrial, civic, hospital/medical, university, entertainment, logistics/warehouse, port, mall complex and military/quarantine zones.

## 2. Destruction

Every structural surface ultimately resolves to micro voxels with a material and integrity value. Weapons apply material-aware damage. The next layer will add support graphs so destroyed columns/floors can detach rubble islands rather than leaving impossible floating buildings.

## 3. Firearms

Weapons are data-driven around caliber, projectile/trace behavior, penetration, recoil, spread, cyclic rate, magazine, reload style, durability, noise and attachment sockets. Gunshots feed the zombie director as sound events. Suppressors reduce range, not eliminate detection.

## 4. Zombie ecology

Zombies exist at three simulation levels: active actors near the player, lightweight agents in nearby streamed districts, and population counters in distant districts. Noise, light, blood, doors, alarms and fires can pull population between cells and create hordes.

Planned archetypes should mostly be behavioral rather than magical: walker, runner, crawler, recently infected, armored/riot, firefighter, soldier, bloated/decayed and rare mutation classes.

## 5. Scavenging and salvage

Almost every recognizable object should have at least one of three uses: use directly, dismantle for materials, or become a construction/crafting ingredient. Example: baseball bat -> melee weapon -> broken into wood; office chair -> furniture/barricade -> metal/plastic; car -> transport -> battery/fuel/wiring/metal.

## 6. Construction

Players can place full prefabs for speed and carve/add individual voxels for detail. Core categories: walls, floors, stairs, doors, windows, ladders, roofs, fences, barricades, traps, power, storage, crafting stations and automated defenses such as turrets.

## 7. RPG progression

Attributes should improve behavior, not only percentages. Examples: Strength allows moving heavy props and forcing doors; Engineering unlocks generator rewiring and turret logic; Medicine identifies infection/trauma states; Scavenging exposes hidden material yields; Gunsmithing enables caliber conversion and advanced repairs; Parkour adds vaulting/climbing routes; Stealth reduces sound generated by movement and interaction.

## 8. Immediate vertical slice

The first playable target is one generated 3x3 or 4x4 urban grid with: first-person movement, destructible walls, one pistol/rifle, one zombie type, procedural buildings with interiors, loot containers, doors, a basic inventory, persistent seed/save, and enough destruction to breach a building from the street.
